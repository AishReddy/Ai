Concepts Explained

Generative AI vs Agentic AI vs AI Agents

A structured breakdown of three terms that often get conflated — what they really mean, how they differ, and why it matters.

Foundation

What is Artificial Intelligence?

Before diving into the distinctions, it helps to anchor on the broadest definition. AI is the umbrella — every system discussed here lives under it.

AI is when machines perform tasks that typically require human intelligence.

This includes pattern recognition, reasoning, language understanding, decision-making, and creativity. The three terms — Generative AI, Agentic AI, and AI Agents — all describe different flavors of that broader capability.

Layer 1

Generative AI (GenAI)

Generative AI is a subset of AI that can produce new content. Unlike older AI that only classified or predicted, GenAI creates. It takes a prompt as input and generates an output — the job ends once the content is delivered.

The Engine Behind GenAI

Machine Learning Models ── LLMs ── Deep Learning Models

Large Language Models (LLMs) are the core technology that powers most GenAI. They process tokens using Attention and Feedforward layers stacked into transformer networks. They sit at the intersection of ML and deep learning — trained on vast datasets to predict and generate text.

LLM Limitation

The Knowledge Cutoff Problem

LLMs are trained on data up to a specific date — after that, they have no awareness of new events. This is called the knowledge cutoff date and is a fundamental limitation of purely generative models.

Why this matters

A GenAI model with a knowledge cutoff can't answer "what's the stock price today" or "who won last night's match." To overcome this, models need access to external tools — which is where Agentic AI enters the picture.

Layer 2

Agentic AI & AI Agents

Agentic AI — or AI Agents — goes beyond generating responses. It can plan, decide, and act in the world. The "agentic" quality refers to agency: the capacity to initiate actions, use tools, and work toward goals over multiple steps without constant human input.

GenAI

Generative AI — responds to prompts

  • ■ Proactive — only reactive
  • ■ High autonomy — human drives
  • ■ Needs tool access (optional)
  • ■ Can execute actions
  • ■ Job ends after one output

Agentic AI

AI Agents — plans and acts autonomously

  • ■ ✓ Proactive — works toward goals
  • ■ ✓ Higher levels of autonomy
  • ■ ✓ Access to internal & external tools
  • ■ ✓ Can perform real-world actions
  • ■ Continues across multiple steps

The key insight

GenAI and Agentic AI aren't in opposition — agents are typically built on top of LLMs. The difference is the layer of autonomy, tools, and action-execution added around the model. Think of GenAI as the brain, and Agentic AI as the brain with hands, eyes, and a to-do list.

Deep Dive

AI Agents — Three Core Pillars

An AI Agent isn't just "smarter GenAI." It has three structural properties that define what makes something an agent. The video uses a real-world example: an Amazon-like shopping agent that can act end-to-end.

1 · Can Perform Actions

An agent doesn't just suggest — it acts. It can browse products, add items to a cart, place orders, and trigger downstream workflows. The action is real, not hypothetical.

2 · Can Access Tools

Agents are wired into systems — for example, a shipping or delivery API. This allows them to check stock, get real-time prices, confirm availability, and track orders without a human doing it manually.

3 · Has Some Autonomy

Agents don't need step-by-step hand-holding. Given a goal, they figure out the path. They may still loop in a human for high-stakes decisions, but for routine execution, they run independently.

The Fine Distinction

AI Agents vs Agentic AI — Not the Same

The video draws a subtle but important line between these two terms. They're related but not interchangeable:

AI Agents

Task-focused

An AI Agent is a specific, scoped system built to accomplish a defined set of tasks. It has a clear job description — think of it as a specialist. One agent, one domain.

Example: An agent that monitors gold prices and places a buy order when they drop below a threshold.

Agentic AI

Goal-focused

Agentic AI describes a broader system design oriented around achieving an overarching goal by coordinating multiple tasks and agents. It's the architecture, not just one agent.

Task 1 Task 2 Task 3

Multiple agents, each handling sub-tasks, working in coordination toward the higher goal.

The analogy

AI Agents are like individual employees with job titles. Agentic AI is like the company they work for — structured, goal-driven, and made up of many agents working in concert.

Where this is all headed

The progression is clear: from AI that answers questions (GenAI) → to AI that does tasks (AI Agents) → to AI that pursues goals across coordinated systems (Agentic AI). We're moving from tools we use to systems that work for us — and the frameworks above are the building blocks making that possible right now.
